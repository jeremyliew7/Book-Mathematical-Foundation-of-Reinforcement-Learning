# 6.1 Update a mean as each sample arrives

**Source:** [Chapter 6 PDF, pages 2–3](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%206%20Stochastic%20Approximation.pdf#page=2), printed pages 102–103. This companion connects the chapter's incremental mean to the decision-room returns from [Chapter 5](05-01.md).

In Chapter 5 we averaged complete returns. Suppose new returns keep arriving: can we update the mean without retaining and summing the entire history each time? Yes. The new mean is the old mean plus a fraction of the new sample's difference from it.

## First fix the index

The source denotes the average of the first $k$ samples by

$$
w_{k+1}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_i.
$$

Thus, for $k\ge2$, $w_k$ is the average of the **first $k-1$** observations. The subscript on $w$ is one ahead of the count of observations it summarizes. The source initializes $w_1=x_1$; the first update then gives $w_2=x_1$.

For instance, after receiving $x_1,x_2,x_3$, the mean is $w_4$, not $w_3$. Write this correspondence down before substituting into the formula.

## Derive the incremental update

The old mean $w_k$ represents $k-1$ observations, so their total is $(k-1)w_k$. Add the new observation $x_k$, then divide by the new count $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w_{k+1}
&=\frac{(k-1)w_k+x_k}{k}\\
&=\left(1-\frac1k\right)w_k+\frac1k x_k\\
&=w_k+\frac1k(x_k-w_k).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first line combines the old total and the new sample. The second distributes the division by $k$. The third groups the old estimate with a correction: $x_k-w_k$ is the signed difference between observation and estimate, and $1/k$ is the weight of that correction. This identity is exact for any finite sequence, regardless of whether the observations are independent.

## Work through three returns

Reuse the illustrative fixed-policy room episodes with returns $x_1=1$, $x_2=3$, and $x_3=0.5$ from [Section 5.1](05-01.md).

| New sample | Update | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $x_1=1$ | $w_2=w_1+1(x_1-w_1)$ | $1$ |
| $x_2=3$ | $w_3=1+\frac12(3-1)$ | $2$ |
| $x_3=0.5$ | $w_4=2+\frac13(0.5-2)$ | $1.5$ |

The batch calculation gives $(1+3+0.5)/3=1.5$, the same result in exact arithmetic. Storing the current mean and count suffices for the update. For independent fixed-policy samples with an existing mean, the law of large numbers supports convergence of the sample average to that mean. The arithmetic identity itself makes no statistical assumption.

**Checkpoint:** When the third observation is below the current mean, what is the sign of the correction? Why is its coefficient $1/3$?

## Generalize the weight

The source next writes

$$
w_{k+1}=w_k+\alpha_k(x_k-w_k),
$$

where $\alpha_k>0$ is the step size. Choosing $\alpha_k=1/k$ recovers the sample mean above. Other choices weight the history differently. For example, starting from $w_2=1$, a constant $\alpha=1/2$ gives $w_3=2$ after sample $3$, then $w_4=1.25$ after sample $0.5$; the ordinary three-sample mean is $1.5$.

A larger step responds more strongly to the latest sample. Convergence of a general stochastic-approximation update depends on the step sizes and assumptions about the samples; Section 6.2 develops those conditions.
