# 6.6 Q&A

### What is stochastic approximation doing?

It uses successive noisy observations to approach a solution of a root-finding or optimization problem. A reading changes the current estimate; a sequence of readings provides information about the underlying average direction.

### Why study mean estimation before reinforcement learning?

A state value is an expected return. Mean estimation introduces the update $w\leftarrow w+\alpha(\text{sample}-w)$, which will reappear in value learning. TD replaces the simple sample target by a reward plus a discounted successor estimate.

### Why can SGD use a sample gradient?

For an expected objective, the gradient is the expectation of the sample gradient when differentiation and expectation can be interchanged. Under suitable sampling, the current sample gradient has that conditional expectation. Step-size, curvature and noise conditions determine what convergence guarantee follows; see [Section 6.4](06-04.md).

### What does a minibatch change?

It averages several sample gradients at the same parameter value. For independent gradients, averaging reduces variance, while additional samples require additional computation. The comparison depends on sampling and cost.

   *Hint:* identify the quantity revised after observing $X_k$.
2. **Apply.** Describe the root and noisy observation used when estimating the mean of $X$ by Robbins–Monro.

   *Hint:* an equation for the desired mean is $w-\mathbb E[X]=0$.
3. **Reason.** Why can an observation with zero unconditional mean still be unsuitable as noise in a history-dependent update?

   *Hint:* examine its conditional mean given the current estimate.
