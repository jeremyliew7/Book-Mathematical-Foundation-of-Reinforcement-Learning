# 2.7 Solving state values from the Bellman equation

You have assembled a Bellman system $v_\pi=r_\pi+\gamma P_\pi v_\pi$. The unknown vector appears on both sides. Is there one solution, and can we reach it by repeatedly updating a guess?

We will solve a tiny system two ways, then prove why the iterative method approaches the same answer. This is **policy evaluation**: the policy stays fixed throughout. It is not yet a method for improving the policy.

## Setup: what is known?

Assume a finite state space, a fixed stationary policy, a known reward vector $r_\pi$, and a known row-stochastic transition matrix $P_\pi$. Let $0<\gamma<1$. These conditions are important for the convergence argument below. The vector $v_\pi$ is the value to find; an approximation $v_k$ is an estimate at iteration $k$, not another name for the exact value.

As an **original worked example**, take two continuing states with

$$
P_\pi=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad
r_\pi=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix},\quad
\gamma=0.5.
$$

Both rows sum to one. The first state moves to the second with reward $0$; the second remains at itself with reward $1$. The numbers are a hand example, not the book's Figure 2.7 and not the output of a program.

## 1. Solve the equations directly

Subtract $\gamma P_\pi v_\pi$ from both sides:

$$
(I-\gamma P_\pi)v_\pi=r_\pi.
$$

If $I-\gamma P_\pi$ is invertible, multiplying by its inverse gives the book's closed form:

$$
v_\pi=(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}r_\pi.
$$

That conditional “if” needs justification: a square matrix is not automatically invertible. Here $\|P_\pi\|_\infty=1$ because its entries are nonnegative and every row sums to one. Therefore $\|\gamma P_\pi\|_\infty=\gamma<1$, and the matrix series converges:

$$
(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}
=I+\gamma P_\pi+\gamma^2P_\pi^2+\cdots.
$$

This is an alternative explanation of invertibility; the source uses Gershgorin discs. Each term is elementwise nonnegative. If two reward vectors satisfy $r_1\geq r_2$ elementwise **with the same $P_\pi$ and $\gamma$**, multiplying by this nonnegative inverse gives $v_1\geq v_2$. If a changed policy also changes $P_\pi$, that comparison does not follow from the reward vectors alone.

### Work through the two-state numbers

Substituting our example gives

$$
I-\gamma P_\pi=
\begin{bmatrix}1&-0.5\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix},\qquad
(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}=
\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

The dimensions are $2\times2$ for both matrices and $2\times1$ for each vector. Multiply the inverse by $r_\pi$:

$$
v_\pi=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Check by substitution: the second value satisfies $2=1+0.5(2)$; the first satisfies $1=0+0.5(2)$. In practice, a numerical linear solver can solve $(I-\gamma P_\pi)v=r_\pi$ without explicitly constructing the inverse.

## 2. Reach the solution through repeated backups

Start from a guess $v_0$ and repeatedly apply the fixed-policy backup:

$$
v_{k+1}=r_\pi+\gamma P_\pi v_k.
$$

For our example, begin with $v_0=[0,0]^T$. Each component of $v_{k+1}$ uses the previous vector $v_k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_1&=[0,1]^T,\\
v_2&=[0.5,1.5]^T,\\
v_3&=[0.75,1.75]^T.
\end{aligned}
$$

For example, the first component of $v_2$ is $0+0.5(1)=0.5$, because the first row of $P_\pi$ selects the second component of $v_1$. These are finite hand calculations. They approach $[1,2]^T$, but none of these first three vectors is the exact solution.

### Why does it converge?

Define the error $e_k=v_k-v_\pi$. Since the exact value obeys the same fixed-point equation, subtracting it from the update cancels the reward term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_{k+1}
&=\bigl(r_\pi+\gamma P_\pi v_k\bigr)
 -\bigl(r_\pi+\gamma P_\pi v_\pi\bigr)\\
&=\gamma P_\pi e_k.
\end{aligned}
$$

The infinity norm $\|e\|_\infty$ is the largest absolute component of $e$. Each component of $P_\pi e$ is a weighted average of error components, so $\|P_\pi e\|_\infty\leq\|e\|_\infty$. Hence

$$
\|e_{k+1}\|_\infty\leq\gamma\|e_k\|_\infty
\leq\gamma^{k+1}\|e_0\|_\infty\longrightarrow0.
$$

The factor $\gamma<1$ makes the upper bound shrink. In the worked example, $\|e_0\|_\infty=2$, while the errors after one and two updates have infinity norms $1$ and $0.5$. The theorem is about the limit; it does not say a finite number of iterations is exact. If $\gamma=1$, this contraction bound no longer proves convergence, even though special undiscounted problems may be solvable under other conditions.

## 3. When can we stop?

A program usually does not know the exact $v_\pi$, so it cannot directly measure $\|e_k\|_\infty$. Let $T(v)=r_\pi+\gamma P_\pi v$ and define the **residual** $d_k=\|T(v_k)-v_k\|_\infty$. Since $T(v_\pi)=v_\pi$, the triangle inequality and contraction give

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|v_k-v_\pi\|_\infty
&\leq\|v_k-T(v_k)\|_\infty+\|T(v_k)-T(v_\pi)\|_\infty\\
&\leq d_k+\gamma\|v_k-v_\pi\|_\infty,
\end{aligned}
$$

so $\|v_k-v_\pi\|_\infty\leq d_k/(1-\gamma)$. It also shows why the same residual can permit a larger error bound when $\gamma$ is close to one.

## 4. What the book's larger grid adds

Figure 2.7 shows fixed policies and their computed value grids for $\gamma=0.9$. Two displayed policies have the same state values even though their selected actions differ in two states. Equal values therefore do not imply identical policies. The figure illustrates specific policies and a specific reward model; it does not claim that all distinct policies have equal values or that an arbitrary iteration count gives an exact numerical answer.

## Practice

1. **One-state system.** A continuing state gives reward $3$ at each step, always returns to itself, and has $\gamma=0.5$. Starting from $v_0=0$, calculate $v_1$ and $v_2$, and write the exact fixed-point equation.

   *Hint:* the update uses the previous value, not the value currently being computed.
2. **Error propagation.** Starting from $e_{k+1}=\gamma P_\pi e_k$, explain why the maximum absolute error cannot grow when $P_\pi$ is row-stochastic and $0<\gamma<1$.

   *Hint:* consider what a probability-weighted average can do to the largest absolute component.
3. **Stopping.** With $\gamma=0.8$ and residual $d_k=0.02$, use the displayed bound to bound the infinity-norm error of $v_k$.

   *Hint:* divide the residual by $1-\gamma$; the bound applies to $v_k$ as defined above.
4. **Change an assumption.** Two policies have different rewards *and* different transition matrices. Why is comparing only their reward vectors insufficient to apply the monotonicity statement in Section 1?

   *Hint:* identify what is held fixed in the nonnegative-inverse comparison.

## Take forward

The closed form and iterative backup solve the same fixed-policy system under the stated conditions. The inverse expresses the solution; the iteration constructs successive approximations. Neither step changes the policy. [Section 2.8](02-08.md) next asks what changes when the first action is specified as well as the state.
