# 3.6 Summary: from policy comparison to value iteration

[Chapter 3 PDF, page 19](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%203%20Optimal%20State%20Values%20and%20Bellman%20Optimality%20Equation.pdf#page=19), printed page 53. Prerequisites: the finite discounted MDP setting and Sections 3.1–3.5.

## Follow the chain of reasoning

1. **Compare policies:** $v_{\pi_1}\ge v_{\pi_2}$ means no lower value in every state, not merely at one selected start state. Two policies can be incomparable ([3.2](03-02.md)).
2. **Compare actions:** for a candidate vector, calculate each action's expected immediate reward plus discounted successor value. A policy-weighted average cannot exceed the largest action score ([3.3](03-03.md)).
3. **Solve a fixed point:** the optimality operator $T$ is a contraction for the stated finite discounted setting. It has a unique fixed point $v^*$, and repeated applications converge to it ([3.3](03-03.md)).
4. **Recover a policy:** a policy greedy for the true $v^*$ achieves that value and is optimal. The value is unique; tied actions can yield multiple optimal policies ([3.4](03-04.md)).
5. **Check the model:** rewards, transitions, and $\gamma$ determine the decision problem. The positive affine invariance result requires the same transformation on every continuing step ([3.5](03-05.md)).

## Revisit one small model

In the hand-worked two-state model of Sections 3.3–3.4, $\gamma=0.5$ and the fixed-point candidate is $(v^*(A),v^*(B))=(1.2,0.4)$. At $A$, Left scores $1+0.5(0.4)=1.2$ and Right scores $0.5+0.5(1.2)=1.1$. Left is therefore greedy for that fixed point; the complete argument for why the policy is optimal is in Section 3.4.

## Review

1. **Recognize:** What does “no worse at every state” require? *Hint:* compare vectors component by component.
2. **Apply:** Which action is greedy at $A$ in the worked model, and what future value did its score use? *Hint:* trace its next state.
3. **Reason:** Why does uniqueness of $v^*$ not guarantee a unique policy? *Hint:* consider tied maximizing actions.
4. **Extend:** Why does a finite iterate $v_k$ need a separate check before its greedy policy is called exactly optimal? *Hint:* convergence of a limit is different from equality after a finite number of updates.
