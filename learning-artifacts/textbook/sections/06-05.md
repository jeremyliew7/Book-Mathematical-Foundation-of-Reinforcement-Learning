# 6.5 Summary

Chapter 6 begins with a simple problem: estimate an unknown mean from observations arriving one by one. It then makes the same update pattern useful for root finding and optimization.

| Problem | Desired target | Observation used in an update |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mean estimation | $w^*=\mathbb E[X]$ | A sample $X_k$ |
| Robbins–Monro | A root $g(w^*)=0$ | A noisy observation of $g(w_k)$ |
| SGD | A minimum of $J(w)=\mathbb E[f(w,X)]$ | A sample gradient $\nabla_w f(w_k,X_k)$ |

For squared loss $f(w,X)=\tfrac12(w-X)^2$, SGD becomes $w_{k+1}=w_k+\alpha_k(X_k-w_k)$, exactly the mean-estimation update. For instance, an estimate $2$, observation $6$, and step size $0.25$ produce $3$. As a Robbins–Monro update, the mean problem uses $g(w)=w-\mathbb E[X]$ and observes $w-X_k$.

The common convergence story requires both a corrective average direction and control of the noise and step sizes. Section 6.2 gives examples of why each stated condition must be checked. Chapter 7 reuses the target-minus-estimate pattern with a new feature: the target contains another current value estimate.

## Review

1. **Identify.** In each table row, what is known after one observation and what is the unknown target?

   *Hint:* distinguish a sample from its expectation.
2. **Calculate.** Update an estimate $3$ with observation $7$ and step size $0.2$; express the same operation as subtracting the squared-loss sample gradient.

   *Hint:* the sample gradient is $w-X$.
3. **Connect.** Why does replacing the sample with $R+\gamma v(S')$ change the analysis?

   *Hint:* ask whether the target depends on a quantity being learned.
