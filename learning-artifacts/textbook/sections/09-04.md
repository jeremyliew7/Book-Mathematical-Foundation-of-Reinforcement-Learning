# 9.4 Monte Carlo policy gradient (REINFORCE)

## Source and scope

[Chapter 9, physical PDF pages 20–22](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%209%20Policy%20Gradient%20Methods.pdf#page=20), book pages 210–212. Equation layout needs visual comparison with the PDF.

## Learning objectives and prerequisites

Understand the policy-gradient identity and discounted returns. Explain the score-function update, how sampled returns can estimate action values, and why the sampling distribution matters.

## The problem

A policy gradient is expressed as an expectation involving an unknown action value. How can an agent update policy parameters using trajectories sampled from the policy instead of computing the exact expectation?

## The concept

REINFORCE is a Monte Carlo policy-gradient method. It uses a sampled return as a noisy signal for a sampled state–action pair and changes the parameters in the direction of a score function. It is a stochastic estimate, not the exact gradient on every sample.

## Derivation and examples

The source gives the general sampled update

$$\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\alpha\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)\,\hat q(S_t,A_t).$$

In its episodic Monte Carlo procedure, $\hat q$ is the discounted return observed from that point to episode end. The score-function identity $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)=\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(a\mid s)/\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$ applies where the action probability is positive and differentiable.

The chapter describes a first-order local interpretation: a sufficiently small step in the score direction with a positive weight tends to increase the sampled action probability when the relevant gradient is nonzero. It is not an unconditional guarantee for an arbitrary finite step, and a negative weight does not make an impossible action sampleable.

The source notes that the state distribution used in a gradient identity and the distribution produced by the practical episode procedure are not automatically identical. An exact estimator depends on the chosen objective, time weighting, sampling distribution, and whether the policy is held fixed while collecting and processing the trajectory. Reusing an episode after changing parameters is not automatically equivalent to fresh on-policy data.

## Applications and limitations

REINFORCE provides a starting point for policy-based control and actor–critic methods. Monte Carlo returns can be noisy and require complete episodes in this formulation. A baseline can reduce variance under suitable conditions, but it must not introduce bias; do not assume every baseline or sampling procedure is valid without checking its dependence and weighting.

## Exercises

1. Why does $\nabla\log\pi$ require attention to action probabilities and differentiability?
2. If rewards are $1,2$ and $\gamma=0.5$, form the return from the first step.
3. Why might repeatedly updating a policy using one fixed old trajectory differ from generating fresh trajectories after each update?

## Key terms and misconceptions

| Term | Meaning | Common misconception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Score function | Gradient of the log probability of a sampled action | It is the reward itself. |
| Monte Carlo return | Discounted sum of subsequent sampled rewards | A single return is an exact action value. |
| On-policy sampling | Samples follow the policy appropriate to the estimator | Old data remain on-policy after arbitrary parameter changes. |
