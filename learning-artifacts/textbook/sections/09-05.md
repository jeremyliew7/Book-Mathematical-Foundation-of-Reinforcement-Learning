# 9.5 Summary

## Learning objectives and prerequisites

Review the policy representations, performance objectives, gradient identities, and Monte Carlo policy-gradient update introduced in Chapter 9.

## The problem

How do the mathematical pieces connect an explicit performance objective to a practical change in policy parameters?

## The concept

Choose a policy family and objective, derive an applicable gradient, and estimate it from data to update the policy. Different discounted and average-reward objectives have different assumptions and sampling distributions; their formulas should not be interchanged without checking those details.

## Derivation and examples

For a differentiable policy, the score-function form combines $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$ with an action-value signal under an appropriate state–action distribution. REINFORCE replaces the value signal with sampled return. The exact identity and normalization depend on the objective established in sections 9.2–9.4.

## Applications and limitations

This summary is a navigation aid and does not establish a proof of the gradient theorem or convergence of a particular implementation. The next chapter introduces actor–critic methods.

## Key terms and misconceptions

| Term | Meaning | Common misconception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy gradient | Gradient of a specified policy objective | All objectives use the same sampling weights. |
| REINFORCE | Monte Carlo policy-gradient method | Every sample equals the exact gradient. |
