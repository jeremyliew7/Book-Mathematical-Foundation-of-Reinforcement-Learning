# 2.10 Chapter review: explain, calculate, and question

**Source:** [Chapter 2 PDF, pages 19–20](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%202%20State%20Values%20and%20Bellman%20Equation.pdf#page=19), printed pages 33–34. The author's Q&A contains answers. To practise independently, use the original prompts below first, then return to the relevant sections or the source.

**Prerequisites:** return and state value ([2.1](02-01.md), [2.3](02-03.md)), the Bellman equation ([2.4](02-04.md)), and the relation between state and action values ([2.8](02-08.md)).

## How to use the questions

Answer in order. For a computation, write the setup, not just the final number. For an explanation, say which assumptions make your statement true. The hints point to a method without giving the answer. If you get stuck, ask the tutor for one hint at a time; compare your reasoning only after your attempt.

## Part 1: identify the question

1. One episode started at $s$ and produced discounted return $G_t=8$. What additional information would you need to determine $v_\pi(s)$? Explain why the observed $8$ may differ from the answer. *Hint:* consider possible continuations and how they are weighted.
2. A classmate says: “The equation for $v_\pi(s)$ has unknown values on its right-hand side, so it cannot be solved.” What system must be written before deciding? *Hint:* consider all states, not one equation in isolation.
3. A different classmate replaces the policy average in the Bellman expectation equation with $\max_a$. What question changed? *Hint:* distinguish evaluating a specified policy from selecting an optimal action.

## Part 2: work through a small MDP

Consider the following **original exercise**, with two continuing states $A$ and $B$, discount $\gamma=0.5$, deterministic outcomes for each listed action, and a fixed stationary policy:

| State and action | Policy probability | Immediate reward | Next state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $A$, `safe` | $0.75$ | $0$ | $B$ |
| $A$, `risky` | $0.25$ | $2$ | $A$ |
| $B$, `wait` | $1$ | $1$ | $B$ |

4. Write and solve the Bellman equation for $v_\pi(B)$. Then write and solve the equation for $v_\pi(A)$. *Hint:* after the first transition, use the value of the next state; weight the two actions at $A$ by their policy probabilities.
5. Calculate $q_\pi(A,\texttt{safe})$ and $q_\pi(A,\texttt{risky})$ using your state values. Check that their policy-weighted average agrees with your answer to question 4. *Hint:* when evaluating an action value, fix that first action before using the continuation value.
6. A third legal action, `detour`, is added at $A$: it gives reward $-1$ and goes to $B$. The current policy assigns it probability zero and does not otherwise change. Write its action value and explain whether it contributes to $v_\pi(A)$ under the current policy. *Hint:* separate the first-action-fixed experiment from the policy average.

## Part 3: test the assumptions

7. Suppose reward and next state are correlated after one action. Does the Bellman equation require them to be independent? Explain how a joint outcome model or separate marginal expectations can be used. *Hint:* distinguish a sum of expectations from a product of probabilities.
8. If the discount were changed to $\gamma=1$ while state $B$ continued giving $+1$ forever, which step in your calculation of $v_\pi(B)$ would need reconsideration? *Hint:* examine the infinite return before treating it as a finite number.
9. Why does comparing two policies only at $A$ not establish a value ordering at every state? Give a situation in which another starting state matters.

## Review route after your attempt

- Trouble separating a realized return from an expectation: revisit [Section 2.1](02-01.md) and [Section 2.3](02-03.md).
- Trouble writing or solving the simultaneous state equations: revisit [Section 2.2](02-02.md) and [Section 2.4](02-04.md).
- Trouble fixing the first action or handling an unchosen action: revisit [Section 2.8](02-08.md).

Do not infer complete understanding from a single score. The useful result is knowing which definition, assumption or step needs another pass.
