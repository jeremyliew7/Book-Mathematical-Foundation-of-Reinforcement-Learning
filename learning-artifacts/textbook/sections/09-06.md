# 9.6 Q&A

## Source and scope

[Chapter 9, physical PDF pages 23–24](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%209%20Policy%20Gradient%20Methods.pdf#page=23), book pages 213–214. The original PDF contains answers; this companion provides independent review prompts without answers. Visual page review is pending.

## Learning objectives and prerequisites

Review the chapter's policy-gradient objectives, score function, and stochastic update after studying sections 9.1–9.4.

## The problem

Can you explain the purpose and limitations of a policy-gradient update without relying on a memorized formula?

## The concept

The source Q&A revisits objective selection, differences between discounted and average-reward formulations, and the use of a log-probability gradient. Consult the individual sections for the assumptions and derivations.

## Derivation and examples

This is a review section rather than a new derivation. Use the source equations and the earlier companions when working through the questions; do not assume that related objectives have identical exact gradients.

## Applications and limitations

The questions provide opportunities to recall and apply the chapter. Answering them is not by itself evidence of a complete proof or an executed reinforcement-learning experiment.

## Exercises

1. Describe the role of an objective and its gradient in a policy-gradient algorithm.
2. Why is the log-probability score useful when rewriting the gradient as an expectation?
3. What must you verify before using samples from a trajectory to estimate a particular objective's gradient?

## Key terms and misconceptions

| Term | Meaning | Common misconception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Objective | Scalar quantity a policy update seeks to improve | Any reasonable objective has the same gradient. |
| Sampling distribution | Distribution that generates an estimator's samples | It never affects estimator correctness. |
