# 10.5 Summary

[Chapter 10 PDF, page 21](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%2010%20Actor-Critic%20Methods.pdf#page=21). This review connects the four algorithms developed in the preceding sections.

## One chapter, four decisions

Every variant pairs an **actor**, which changes the policy, with a **critic**, which estimates a value-related learning signal. To understand a particular update, identify the actor's output, the critic's target, the policy that generated the transition, and the state weighting needed for the objective.

| Variant | Actor signal | Critic signal | Data and key condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [QAC](10-01.md) | score times $q_w(s,a)$ | Sarsa TD error for $q_w$ | the book samples the next action from the current policy |
| [A2C](10-02.md) | score times TD error | TD error for $v_w$ | a state-only baseline preserves the expected score signal |
| [Off-policy A2C](10-03.md) | action ratio times TD error and score | weighted TD error | target actions need behavior support; state weighting requires separate care |
| [Deterministic actor-critic](10-04.md) | critic action derivative through the actor | TD error with target actor's next action | differentiability and suitable behavior-state/action coverage |

The same transition can play different roles. In deterministic actor-critic, the actual behavior action produced the reward and next state; the actor's next-state action appears in the critic target. In stochastic off-policy A2C, a target-to-behavior action ratio adjusts the conditional action contribution. Neither fact alone establishes that sampled states match the weighting of a particular exact gradient.

## Retrieval practice

1. A critic estimates $v_w(s)$ and the actor multiplies its score by $r+\gamma v_w(s')-v_w(s)$. Which chapter algorithm does this resemble, and when does its expected TD error equal advantage?
2. Suppose a dataset was collected by another policy. Identify the support condition required for action importance sampling and explain why state weighting remains a separate question.
3. An actor outputs a continuous action rather than probabilities. Identify the two derivatives that form its local policy update.
