# 10.1 The simplest actor-critic algorithm (QAC)

[Chapter 10 PDF, page 2](../../../3%20-%20Chapter%2010%20Actor-Critic%20Methods.pdf#page=2). Prerequisites: a stochastic policy, a policy-gradient score, Sarsa and TD error.

## A policy needs feedback

Imagine a robot repeatedly choosing **left** or **right**. It can adjust a parameter to make left more or less likely, but how should it know whether an action is worth encouraging? Waiting for an entire episode gives a return, as in REINFORCE. Q actor-critic instead learns an action-value estimate from one-step transitions and uses it to guide the policy.

The **actor** is the policy $\pi_\theta(a\mid s)$; it produces actions and adjusts $\theta$. The **critic** is a function $q_w(s,a)$; it estimates the return after taking $a$ in $s$ and following the current policy thereafter. The two parameters need not have the same dimension.

## From a score to a learning step

The policy-gradient expression from Chapter 9 contains a state-weighted expectation of the score $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(A\mid S)$ times the true action value $q_{\pi_\theta}(S,A)$. Its exact interpretation depends on the chosen objective and state weights; see [Section 9.3](09-03.md). In QAC, the critic supplies $q_w$ in place of the unknown value, and the observed $(s_t,a_t)$ supplies one sample:

$$
\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\alpha_\theta\nabla_\theta\log\pi_{\theta_t}(a_t\mid s_t)q_{w_t}(s_t,a_t).
$$

The score tells us how changing $\theta$ changes the sampled action's log probability. The critic determines how strongly the actor adjusts in that direction. A particular sampled update can point away from the expected update; an approximate critic also changes the expected signal.

The critic needs its own target. The book's Algorithm 10.1 selects $a_{t+1}$ from the current policy at $s_{t+1}$, then forms

$$
\delta_t=r_{t+1}+\gamma q_{w_t}(s_{t+1},a_{t+1})-q_{w_t}(s_t,a_t),\quad
w_{t+1}=w_t+\alpha_w\delta_t\nabla_wq_{w_t}(s_t,a_t).
$$

The target combines the observed reward and the estimated value of the actual *next-policy action*. This is Sarsa. The critic treats the target as fixed during this semi-gradient update. At a terminal transition the future-value term is zero.

## Worked transition

Use a binary policy $\pi_\theta(L\mid s)=\sigma(\theta)$ and $\pi_\theta(R\mid s)=1-\sigma(\theta)$, where $\sigma(\theta)=1/(1+e^{-\theta})$. At $\theta=0$, both actions have probability $0.5$ and the left-action score is $1-\sigma(0)=0.5$.

Suppose $L$ was chosen, $q_w(s,L)=2$, reward $r=1$, discount $\gamma=0.9$, and the next sampled action has $q_w(s',a')=3$. Let both learning rates equal $0.1$ and let the current tabular critic entry be the parameter being updated.

1. Actor: $\theta_{t+1}=0+0.1(0.5)(2)=0.1$. The policy now assigns left probability $\sigma(0.1)\approx0.525$.
2. Critic target: $1+0.9(3)=3.7$; TD error: $3.7-2=1.7$.
3. Since the tabular entry has derivative $1$, its new value is $2+0.1(1.7)=2.17$.

Both updates use the old critic value, as in the book's displayed simultaneous updates. The observed target exceeds the current estimate, so the critic moves upward; the positive actor signal increases the selected action's probability.
